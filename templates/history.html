History of Autism Research

From misunderstanding to acceptance — the journey of autism awareness.

1911

The Word is Coined

Swiss psychiatrist Eugen Bleuler first used the term "autism" (from the Greek "autos" meaning self) to describe a symptom of schizophrenia involving social withdrawal.

1943

Leo Kanner's Study

American psychiatrist Leo Kanner published a landmark paper describing 11 children with "early infantile autism" — the first formal definition of autism as a separate condition from schizophrenia.

1944

Hans Asperger's Work

Austrian pediatrician Hans Asperger independently described a milder form of autism — later called Asperger Syndrome — in children who had normal intelligence but social difficulties.

1960s

The "Refrigerator Mother" Theory

Psychologist Bruno Bettelheim wrongly blamed cold, unloving mothers for causing autism. This harmful theory was later completely disproven but caused enormous damage to families for decades.

1980

DSM-III Recognition

Autism was officially added to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-III) as a separate diagnosis — a major milestone in recognizing autism as a real neurological condition.

1994

Asperger Syndrome Added

Asperger Syndrome was added to the DSM-IV, expanding understanding of the autism spectrum to include high-functioning individuals without significant language delays.

2000s

Vaccine Controversy Debunked

A 1998 paper falsely claiming a link between the MMR vaccine and autism was fully retracted. Dozens of large-scale studies involving millions of children confirmed vaccines do not cause autism.

2013

Unified Spectrum — DSM-5

The DSM-5 unified all autism subtypes (Asperger Syndrome, PDD-NOS, Childhood Disintegrative Disorder) under the single umbrella term "Autism Spectrum Disorder" (ASD).

Today

AI & Early Detection

Machine learning models like AutismAI are now being used to assist in early screening. Research continues into genetic markers, brain imaging, and behavioural analysis to improve detection and support.
